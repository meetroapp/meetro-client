## Core Two-Gate Constitutional Test

### Meetro Constitution Gate

- [ ] Does this preserve dignity?
- [ ] Does this strengthen or responsibly steward relationships?
- [ ] Is it fair?
- [ ] Is it transparent?
- [ ] Does it protect trust?
- [ ] Does it serve Meetro's purpose?

### Platform Constitution Gate

- [ ] What is canonical?
- [ ] Who owns it?
- [ ] Who may mutate it?
- [ ] What lifecycle governs it?
- [ ] How is authorization enforced?
- [ ] What privacy boundary applies?
- [ ] What transaction protects it?
- [ ] What prevents duplicates?
- [ ] What evidence certifies it?
- [ ] What deployment proves it?

These results must be recorded independently. The expanded checklist below
tests the evidence behind each answer.

The combined result is APPROVED only when both gates record PASS, BLOCKED when
either records FAIL, and NOT COMPLETE when either is CONDITIONAL or NOT
REVIEWED.

## C. Platform Constitution Gate

### Scope and Durability

- [ ] Each article states a durable engineering principle, not a current
      feature, filename, endpoint, provider, or milestone.
- [ ] Normative terms are used consistently with the Canonical Definitions.
- [ ] Current evidence and reference cases are not promoted into universal law.
- [ ] The text applies coherently across ordinary requests, Emergency,
      relationships, conversations, media, intelligence, and future domains.
- [ ] The constitution can survive reasonable technology and provider changes.

### Truth, Authority, Ownership, and Identity

- [ ] Every authoritative state has one named authority and owner.
- [ ] Authenticated identity cannot be overridden by client input.
- [ ] Canonical IDs are distinct from labels, routes, aliases, and projections.
- [ ] Unknown, missing, stale, or malformed evidence fails closed.
- [ ] A projection cannot create, promote, or rewrite authoritative state.
- [ ] Browser state is limited to replaceable local interaction state.

### Lifecycle, Relationships, and Transactions

- [ ] Lifecycles have explicit initial, allowed, invalid, and terminal states.
- [ ] Transitions are authorized, preconditioned, atomic, and auditable.
- [ ] Idempotency and duplicate-command behavior are defined.
- [ ] Concurrency cannot produce contradictory active relationships or states.
- [ ] A read cannot conceal business mutation.
- [ ] Relationship, conversation, participant, and access authority remain
      canonical and owner-scoped.

### Events, Notifications, Attention, and Time

- [ ] Canonical events follow authoritative transitions rather than client
      invention.
- [ ] Event identity, ordering, provenance, and replay semantics are clear.
- [ ] Notifications are projections and cannot establish delivery, read, or
      lifecycle truth without authoritative evidence.
- [ ] Attention is deduplicated, prioritized, owner-scoped, and recoverable.
- [ ] Device registration and push delivery have explicit authorities.
- [ ] Authoritative time comes from a trusted server or canonical event source.

### Privacy, Security, Media, AI, and Providers

- [ ] Private data is owner-scoped, minimized, redacted, and allowlisted in
      projections.
- [ ] Public exposure requires explicit authorization and purpose.
- [ ] Secrets and protected keys remain outside logs, source, events, analytics,
      and client authority.
- [ ] Media ownership, upload, deletion, and projection contracts are explicit.
- [ ] AI output remains subordinate to canonical facts and authorized human or
      system decisions.
- [ ] External providers cannot become constitutional or domain authority.

### Certification, Deployment, Exceptions, and Change

- [ ] Evidence requirements distinguish source, test, runtime, staging, and
      production proof.
- [ ] Environment, service, artifact, schema, and configuration identities are
      verified before mutation.
- [ ] Commit, push, migration, staging, production, and authenticated testing
      are independently authorized.
- [ ] Rollback compatibility and provenance are mandatory for high-risk change.
- [ ] Exceptions are explicit, bounded, owned, reviewed, and expiring.
- [ ] Amendments preserve history and require challenge and ratification.
- [ ] Ratification itself does not authorize runtime adoption.

## D. Definitions and Invariants

- [ ] All defined terms used normatively have one coherent meaning.

- [ ] No two invariants require contradictory behavior.
