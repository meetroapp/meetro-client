## Decision 1 — Initial Human Constitutional Authority

**Initial Human Constitutional Authority:** William E. Molina, Meetro Founder
and Vision Keeper.

This appointment applies to the initial constitutional process unless later
governance extends or supersedes it. The Human Constitutional Authority:

- represents only the Meetro Constitution gate;
- evaluates purpose, dignity, fairness, relationships, trust, stewardship,
  ethics, human consequences, and product philosophy;

- cannot use the human decision as evidence that the platform gate passed.

## Decision 2 — Initial Platform Constitutional Authority

**Initial Platform Constitutional Authority:** William E. Molina, acting in a
separately recorded Platform Constitutional capacity.

This founding-phase appointment is bounded to canonical truth, authority,
ownership, lifecycle, privacy, authentication, authorization, transaction

The Platform Constitutional Authority must issue a conclusion, evidence record,
and reasoning separate from the human gate. It cannot override the Meetro

authorize runtime work without a separate milestone.

## Decision 3 — Initial Platform Constitutional Steward

**Initial Platform Constitutional Steward:** William E. Molina.

The Steward role is administrative and custodial. It may:

- preserve the frozen candidate and superseded identities;
- maintain governance records and verify hashes;
- open or close governed review periods only after explicit authorization;
- receive and preserve objections and responses;
- identify missing evidence and process violations;
- record decisions without merging constitutional gates; and
- escalate unresolved or conflicted matters.

The Steward does not thereby gain authority to declare ratification, amend the
frozen candidate, override either gate, authorize implementation or production,
remove objections without an attributable response, or adjudicate a personal
conflict without disclosure and required recusal.

## Decision 4 — Initial Challenge Duration

The initial challenge duration is **seven calendar days**.

This decision governs the planned initial challenge period. It does not ratify
seven days as permanent constitutional law. The challenge starts only through a
later explicit opening authorization and opening record containing the exact
opening timestamp, closing timestamp, timezone, frozen hash, objection channel,
named reviewers, conflict disclosures, and evidence-custody location.

After opening, the period may be extended for a material late objection,
missing evidence, newly discovered reviewer conflict, or a blocker requiring
additional examination. It must not be shortened after opening.

## Decision 7 — Recusal and Conflict Rules

The Founder-authorship and initial role-concentration conflict is disclosed
above and mitigated through separate records and independent challenge.

Mandatory recusal applies when:

- a decision concerns misconduct or a governance violation by the reviewer;
- a reviewer has a direct financial or personal conflict beyond ordinary
  founder interest;
- a reviewer would adjudicate their own appointment, removal, or sanction; or
- independent review is expressly required.

When required recusal creates a vacancy, the affected process pauses until an
interim non-conflicted reviewer is appointed and recorded. No objection may be
hidden or removed because it concerns a current authority or reviewer.

### Opening

A separately authorized opening record must:

1. Reverify the frozen candidate and freeze-record hashes.
2. Confirm both constitutional authorities and the Steward.
3. Name the active human, platform, and independent challenge reviewers.
4. Record conflicts, required recusals, and replacement reviewers.
5. Confirm public and sensitive evidence custody.
6. Create the objection directory or append-only register.
7. Record exact opening and closing timestamps and timezone.
8. State the seven-calendar-day duration.
9. Explicitly declare the challenge period open.

### Closing

A separately governed closing record must:

1. Reverify the frozen hash and confirm no candidate change.
2. Inventory every objection and response.
3. Record every disposition and appeal.
4. Identify unresolved blockers and conflicts.
5. Decide whether revision is required.
6. Decide whether ratification review may begin.
7. Preserve all challenge evidence and supersession links.

This record neither opens nor closes the challenge.
