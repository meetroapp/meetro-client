# 6. CQ-004 — VERSION POLICY

## Question

What version model should govern constitutional changes?

## Advisory recommendation

Adopt:

```text
MAJOR.MINOR.REVISION
```

### Major

Use for changes to:

* constitutional authority;
* rights or obligations;
* dual-gate governance;
* amendment or ratification procedure;
* exception or emergency power;
* constitutional scope;
* article removal or fundamental replacement.

Example:

```text
1.0.0 → 2.0.0
```

### Minor

Use for:

* a new article;
* a new universal invariant;
* a materially expanded obligation;
* a new governance process;
* a new category of constitutionally governed system.

Example:

```text
1.0.0 → 1.1.0
```

### Revision

Use for:

* clarification;
* evidence correction;
* terminology alignment;
* non-substantive wording repair;
* link or citation repair;
* correction that does not change obligations or authority.

Example:

```text
1.0.0 → 1.0.1
```

Every version change should preserve:

* prior version;
* prior hash;
* reason;
* classification;
* reviewer decision;
* effective date;
* affected systems.

## Objection decision

```text
NO FORMAL OBJECTION RECOMMENDED
```

# 7. CQ-005 — DUAL-GATE CONFLICT PROCESS

## Question

Does the candidate adequately prevent one Constitution from overriding the other?

## Advisory recommendation

Adopt the frozen candidate’s model:

```text
Both PASS → APPROVED
Either FAIL → BLOCKED
Either CONDITIONAL or NOT REVIEWED → NOT COMPLETE
```

Each gate should possess an independent veto over approval within its own constitutional jurisdiction.

A veto should not mean the gate can rewrite the other Constitution.

Persistent deadlock should permit only:

* subsystem revision;
* scope reduction;
* deferral;
* rejection;
* constitutional amendment.

It must never default to:

* founder preference;
* engineering convenience;
* product urgency;
* financial pressure;
* majority averaging between unrelated gates.

When one gate’s remediation creates risk for the other, both gates must review again.

## Objection decision

```text
NO FORMAL OBJECTION RECOMMENDED
```

The frozen model is the correct protection against both technically correct harm and well-intentioned engineering falsehood.

# 8. CQ-006 — EMERGENCY SUSPENSION AUTHORITY

## Question

Should temporary constitutional-process suspension be allowed?

## Advisory recommendation

```text
ALLOW ONLY BOUNDED PROTECTIVE ACTION
```

Emergency authority may be used only to:

* prevent imminent human harm;
* contain an active security incident;
* stop ongoing data exposure;
* disable a dangerous production capability;
* preserve canonical evidence;
* maintain essential service availability while avoiding greater harm.

Emergency authority must not:

* ratify;
* permanently amend;
* erase evidence;
* create unrelated product scope;
* waive privacy permanently;
* bypass retrospective review;
* convert temporary action into precedent automatically.

## Issuing authority

The Platform Constitutional Authority or a formally appointed incident authority may issue a suspension affecting engineering operations.

A human-safety action affecting the Meetro Constitution must also be reviewed by the Human Constitutional Authority.

## Duration

Recommended:

```text
Shortest operationally necessary duration
Mandatory initial review within 24 hours
Daily review while active
Automatic expiration after seven days unless separately renewed
```

A renewal requires new evidence and a recorded decision.

## Objection decision

```text
NO FORMAL OBJECTION RECOMMENDED
```

The candidate direction is sound, but ratification should include the seven-day automatic expiration.

# 9. CQ-007 — EXISTING-SYSTEM TRANSITION

## Question

How should existing Meetro systems transition after ratification?

## Advisory recommendation

Use these classifications:

```text
COMPLIANT
CONDITIONALLY COMPLIANT
CONSTITUTIONAL DEBT
BLOCKED
NOT YET REVIEWED
```

### Compliant

Evidence proves both gates pass.

### Conditionally compliant

The subsystem may operate under a bounded, recorded condition that does not permit a high-risk authority, privacy, or lifecycle violation.

### Constitutional debt

A known violation exists and must be governed through remediation or exception.

### Blocked

The subsystem contains risk serious enough that continued activation or release is not constitutionally acceptable.

### Not yet reviewed

No compliance conclusion may be inferred.

## No-grace-period defects

The following should ordinarily receive no launch grace period:

* unauthorized data access;
* cross-account leakage;
* client-created canonical authority;
* invalid terminal-state mutation;
* unknown production schema where required for safe operation;
* deployment without identifiable provenance;
* authentication bypass;
* irreversible financial or legal mutation without authority.

## Transition priority

Prioritize by:

1. Human safety.
2. Privacy and authorization.
3. Canonical lifecycle integrity.
4. Transaction and idempotency risk.
5. Production reachability.
6. Number of affected users.
7. Dependency impact.

## Objection decision

```text
NO FORMAL OBJECTION RECOMMENDED
```

# 11. CQ-009 — EVIDENCE CUSTODY

## Question

How should constitutional evidence be preserved?

## Advisory recommendation

Use a two-tier custody model.

### Version-controlled governance evidence

Store in:

```text
docs/PlatformGovernance/
```

Examples:

* freeze records;
* candidate hashes;
* review reports;
* objections;
* responses;
* decisions;
* version histories;
* supersession records;
* public compliance evidence.

### Restricted evidence

Store in access-controlled custody.

Examples:

* production database evidence;
* private user records;
* credentials;
* security findings;
* deployment secrets;
* personal reviewer information;
* privileged legal evidence.

A public governance record should contain only:

* evidence ID;
* owner;
* type;
* date;
* access class;
* hash where appropriate;
* verification result;
* related milestone;
* retention rule.

## Custodian departure

When a custodian leaves:

1. Inventory evidence.
2. Verify hashes.
3. Transfer access.
4. revoke prior access;
5. Record successor custody.
6. Preserve the transfer record.

## Deletion

Constitutional evidence should not be deleted merely because it is inconvenient or superseded.

Private evidence may be deleted according to lawful retention requirements, but the bounded governance record should preserve that it existed and how it was dispositioned.

## Objection decision

```text
NO FORMAL OBJECTION RECOMMENDED
```

# 12. CQ-010 — DOMAIN-CONSTITUTION HIERARCHY

## Question

Should Meetro permit subordinate constitutions or domain charters?

## Advisory recommendation

```text
YES, UNDER EXPLICIT HIERARCHY
```

Recommended hierarchy:

```text
Meetro Vision
│
├── Meetro Constitution
│
└── Meetro Platform Constitution
     │
     ├── Registered Domain Constitution
     ├── Registered Domain Charter
     ├── Platform Standard
     └── Feature Governance
```

A domain instrument may:

* impose stricter obligations;
* define domain-specific authority;
* define domain-specific lifecycle;
* define additional evidence or certification;
* define specialized review procedures.

It must not:

* weaken either Constitution;
* override canonical platform authority;
* reduce privacy or authorization;
* create unreviewed runtime authority;
* redefine shared constitutional terms incompatibly;
* silently supersede another registered domain.

## Registration

Each domain instrument should identify:

* name;
* scope;
* owner;
* governing constitutional articles;
* authority boundaries;
* conflicts;
* version;
* effective date;
* review authority;
* amendment process.

## Change classification

A domain change requires Platform constitutional review when it alters:

* shared canonical authority;
* cross-domain ownership;
* platform privacy;
* shared lifecycle;
* shared deployment or certification;
* another domain’s contract.

## Intelligence Judge

The Intelligence Judge should remain part of an Intelligence-domain governance instrument, not universal constitutional law.
