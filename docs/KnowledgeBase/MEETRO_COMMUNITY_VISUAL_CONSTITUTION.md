# Meetro Community Visual Constitution

Date: July 6, 2026

Status: Official visual identity foundation

## Purpose

This document defines the first unified visual identity for Meetro Community.

It is not a redesign.

It is not a new component system.

It is the shared visual constitution that helps every major surface feel like it belongs to the same neighborhood.

The goal is recognition before explanation. A member should feel Meetro Community before reading the page title.

## 1. Visual Philosophy

Meetro Community should feel warm, human, trustworthy, calm, professional, and local.

The product should not feel like a marketplace, a social feed, a corporate dashboard, a game, or a generic startup app.

The emotional benchmark is the Welcome Experience:

- Morning sunlight.
- Natural wood.
- Warm cream.
- Soft sage.
- Forest green.
- Paper.
- Coffee.
- Conversation.
- Homes.
- Trust.
- Professional guidance.
- Calm confidence.

The guiding visual sentence is:

You are home.

## 2. Emotional Design Principles

Relationships before features.

People before products.

Guidance before decoration.

Confidence before spectacle.

Warmth before novelty.

Clarity before density.

Every screen should quietly answer:

- Where am I?
- What matters here?
- What is moving forward?
- Who is this helping?
- Why does this work matter?

## 3. Primary Color Palette

The primary Meetro Community palette is warm and grounded.

| Token | Color | Meaning | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forest | `#1f4d34` | Trust, primary action, selected state | Primary buttons, selected cards, confident accents |
| Deep Forest | `#14351f` | Depth, professional confidence | Work Center heroes, dark navigation anchors |
| Warm Cream | `#fbf6ed` | Comfort, page warmth | App backgrounds, calm sections |
| Paper | `#fffdf8` | Readability, printed memory | Cards, panels, content surfaces |
| Soft Sage | `#eef4ea` | Community calm | Empty states, selected soft surfaces |

### Purple Retirement Rule

Purple no longer functions as a core Meetro Community interaction color.

Interaction hierarchy:

- Forest moves work forward.
- Charcoal marks the current selection.
- Sage, cream, and paper keep supporting states calm.
- Amber communicates pending guidance.
- Red communicates emergency, error, destructive, or blocked states.

Use forest for primary workflow actions such as continuing, saving, sending,
scheduling, creating, or completing work.

Use charcoal or near-black for selected tabs, segmented controls, active
filters, and utility selections where the control is answering "which view is
current?" rather than "what work moves forward?"

Use sage or paper with a forest border for selected states when charcoal would
feel too heavy.

Approved purple exceptions:

- Companion ambient presence may keep a limited purple glow because it is a
  distinct living presence rather than a core workflow control.
- Explicitly documented premium, founding, or experimental accents may use
  purple sparingly when they are not primary actions, selected tabs, filters,
  chips, badges, links, forms, or workflow CTAs.

Purple should not appear as the default color for primary buttons, selected
tabs, segmented controls, filter pills, utility badges, links, hero gradients,
or form focus states on core Meetro Community surfaces.

### Auth Brand Identity Rule

Public-facing introduction and auth screens should use the full Meetro
Community wordmark or the lantern guidance symbol when a symbol is needed.

Avoid isolated decorative `M` monograms on auth welcome, login, signup, or
language-entry surfaces unless the `M` is the official app icon/favicon in an
explicit app-icon context.

The auth hierarchy should read:

- Meetro Community identity.
- The return/continuity message.
- Join or welcome-back card.
- Legal links.
- Optional support or Companion presence later.

### Public Welcome Page Standard

The public landing page should introduce Meetro Community's philosophy before features.

Use Meetro Community as the canonical public name.

The public page should feel like an invitation into a trusted neighborhood:

- Forest, deep forest, warm cream, paper, sage, wood, coffee, lantern gold, soft
  borders, and soft shadows.
- A relationship-first headline before any product explanation.
- The journey from relationships to communication, understanding, decisions,
  work, history, and stronger relationships.
- Ask Meetro represented through guidance, presence, language, and light.

Do not present Ask Meetro as a cartoon face or mascot.

Avoid generic marketplace, SaaS launch, category-first, AI-first, neon, or
purple-platform language on the public Welcome page.

## 4. Secondary Palette

Secondary colors should support meaning and not compete with the primary palette.

- Purple remains available only for documented Companion presence or premium/accent exceptions.
- Blue remains available for neutral information.
- Green remains the preferred connected, saved, available, or primary selected state.
- Orange remains the preferred warmth and reflection accent.
- Red remains reserved for emergency, destructive, or blocked states.

Do not make every surface purple.

Do not let secondary color become decorative noise.

## 5. Background Colors

Backgrounds should feel like warm paper and morning light.

Preferred:

- Warm cream gradients.
- Paper-white content areas.
- Soft sage endings.
- Very subtle radial warmth behind major surfaces.

Avoid:

- Cold gray pages as the default emotional foundation.
- Heavy blue or purple gradients on general community surfaces.
- Dark backgrounds unless the surface needs professional confidence or photography contrast.

## 6. Surface Colors

Surfaces should feel calm and touchable.

Use:

- `rgba(255, 253, 248, 0.94)` for primary cards.
- `rgba(251, 246, 237, 0.92)` for warm notes.
- `rgba(238, 244, 234, 0.9)` for selected or reassuring states.
- A quiet warm border: `rgba(78, 68, 55, 0.12)`.

Avoid:

- Harsh white boxes stacked inside other boxes.
- Heavy borders.
- High-contrast dashboard panels unless the page is explicitly a professional command surface.

## 7. Button Standards

Primary action buttons should feel confident and grounded.

Preferred:

- Forest green fill.
- White or paper text.
- Rounded pill or soft capsule shape.
- Subtle shadow.
- Verb-first label.

Secondary buttons should feel available but not loud.

Preferred:

- Paper or warm cream surface.
- Forest or ink text.
- Soft border.

Do not use a bright color simply because a button exists.

Do not change workflow ownership to make a button easier to style. The owning surface still owns the work.

## 8. Card Standards

Cards should feel like a trusted record or a warm invitation.

Use cards for:

- Repeated objects.
- Relationship rows.
- Moment cards.
- Professional previews.
- Work items.
- Temporary context.

Avoid using cards for every page section.

Standard card behavior:

- Radius: 20px to 28px.
- Border: warm, low-contrast.
- Shadow: soft and lifted only when it improves hierarchy.
- Padding: enough for touch and translation.
- Background: paper, warm cream, or soft sage.

## 9. Photography Standards

Photography must follow the Meetro Emotional Storytelling Standard.

Lead with life after the work.

Preferred:

- Families.
- Conversation.
- Trust.
- Home life.
- Community.
- Professional guidance.
- Celebration.
- Relationships.

Use finished spaces only when they support the human story.

Avoid leading with:

- Tools.
- Equipment.
- Empty rooms.
- Construction-only imagery.
- Workers posing without context.
- Technical completion.

Every image should answer:

What became possible because this work was completed?

## 10. Typography Standards

Typography should feel human, calm, and readable.

Page titles should be confident and plain.

Section titles should guide the eye.

Body text should reduce uncertainty.

Editorial surfaces such as Meetro Moments may use warmer, more reflective phrasing, but they should remain readable and direct.

Avoid:

- Oversized type inside dense tools.
- Negative letter spacing.
- Label-heavy admin language.
- Software explanations where a human sentence would be clearer.

## 11. Spacing Standards

Spacing should create breathing room without wasting space.

Preferred rhythm:

- 12px to 16px inside compact controls.
- 18px to 24px inside cards.
- 24px to 32px between major sections.
- Larger visual pauses only around hero/reflection surfaces.

Desktop should improve composition, not simply scale cards.

Mobile should remain focused and scroll naturally.

## 12. Corner Radius Standards

Rounded shapes should feel soft, not playful.

Guidance:

- Small controls: 12px to 16px.
- Cards: 20px to 28px.
- Hero/reflection surfaces: 28px to 34px.
- Pills and chips: 999px.

Do not use sharp enterprise rectangles for Meetro Community surfaces.

Do not make every surface a bubble.

## 13. Shadow Standards

Shadows should communicate depth quietly.

Preferred:

- Warm, low-opacity shadows.
- Lift only for cards that sit above the page.
- Inner highlights only on glass or temporary surfaces.

Avoid:

- Heavy floating cards.
- Dark, cold shadows.
- Multiple nested shadows competing for attention.

## 14. Icon Standards

Icons should help recognition without becoming the design.

Preferred:

- Rounded icon capsules.
- Forest, sage, wood, and paper tones.
- Lucide-style consistency where available.
- Decorative icons only when they support meaning.

Avoid:

- Random icon colors.
- Technical symbols for emotional surfaces.
- Icons that compete with content.

## 15. Hero Section Standards

Hero sections should orient, not advertise.

Welcome Experience:

- Emotional benchmark.
- Human photography.
- Warm copy.
- Guided first step.

Community:

- Should feel like shared discovery.
- Show meaningful previews quickly.
- Avoid empty launcher behavior.

Meetro Moments:

- Should feel like preservation.
- Photography and copy should make completed work feel remembered.
- No social/feed behavior.

Work Center:

- Should feel professionally confident.
- Use forest/deep forest when a command surface needs focus.
- Do not turn it into a noisy dashboard.

## 16. Empty State Standards

Empty states should feel like beginnings, not absence.

Preferred:

- Warm surface.
- Short title.
- One helpful sentence.
- One clear next action only when the owner surface can act.

Avoid:

- Empty white space.
- Technical placeholders.
- "No data" language.
- Fake records disguised as user data.

## 17. Accessibility Notes

Visual warmth must never reduce usability.

Requirements:

- Text over photography needs a dark gradient or strong contrast layer.
- Buttons need visible focus states.
- Icons cannot be the only source of meaning.
- Color cannot be the only status signal.
- Touch targets remain comfortable on iPhone.
- Desktop controls remain keyboard accessible.
- Companion and temporary cards must not cover primary actions.

## 18. Examples of Good Consistency

Welcome Experience:

- Strong emotional benchmark.
- Human-centered photography.
- Warm cream and forest tone.

Community:

- Shared destination for all roles.
- Progressive preview sections preserve discovery before choice.

Meetro Moments:

- Preservation language is now explicit.
- Verified history remains distinct from Spotlight discovery.

Work Center:

- Professional confidence direction is correct.
- Needs continued color unification before broad release polish.

Companion:

- Presence model is correct.
- Purple glow remains appropriate because Companion is a distinct living presence.

## 19. Examples of Future Improvements

Documented only for future work:

- Replace remaining broad purple primary actions with the forest action system where they are not Companion or legacy brand-specific.
- Apply the visual tokens to Community, Work Center, Communication Center, and Moments page containers gradually.
- Audit all large photography against the Living Photography standard.
- Reduce cold gray page backgrounds where they are not serving utility.
- Align Work Center professional hero treatment with the deep forest confidence benchmark.
- Keep desktop navigation calm while preventing label truncation.

These should be intentional passes, not opportunistic rewrites.

## 20. Visual Do / Don't

Do:

- Use warm cream, paper, forest, sage, wood, and coffee.
- Lead with people and relationships.
- Make cards feel like records, memories, or trusted work.
- Use depth quietly.
- Let wide screens improve composition.
- Preserve mobile behavior.
- Keep workflows intact.

Don't:

- Make Meetro feel like a marketplace.
- Make Community feel like a directory.
- Make Moments feel like a feed.
- Make Work Center feel like enterprise admin software.
- Make Companion look like generic chat software.
- Add visual noise because space exists.
- Change ownership, routing, data, or workflow to satisfy a visual preference.

## Safe Improvements Implemented

- Forest and deep forest primary colors.
- Warm cream, paper, and sage surfaces.
- Wood and coffee accents.
- Warm border and shadow standards.
- Community page, hero, surface, button, and empty-state utility classes.

The existing shared selected-card utilities now use the forest/sage system instead of a purple-only selected state.

Protected behavior:

- No Home architecture changed.
- No navigation changed.
- No routing changed.
- No project-card workflow changed.
- No Continue Conversation behavior changed.
- No Community entry behavior changed.
- No language.js changes were required.

Recommendations documented only:

### Communication Center Wonder Pass

Protected behavior:

- No routes changed.
- No Continue Conversation behavior changed.
- No ConversationThread routing changed.
- No hiring, emergency, customer, or project conversation ownership changed.
- No backend, API, database, notification, or read/unread logic changed.

Communication principle:

- Communication Center answers: "How do we understand each other?"
- Community begins relationships.
- Communication deepens them.

Recommendations documented only:

Protected behavior:

- No Profile routes changed.
- No Settings functionality changed.

- No backend, API, database, navigation, language, or accessibility behavior changed.

Identity principle:

Recommendations documented only:
