# Constitutional Discovery Framework

Date: July 6, 2026

Status: Permanent editorial gate for future Constitution work

## Purpose

This framework protects The Meetro Constitution from growing simply because new ideas are discovered.

A discovery is not automatically a constitutional principle.

Many discoveries belong elsewhere.

The Constitution should become lighter as it matures.

Not heavier.

## Editorial Mission

Every future discovery must be classified before it is adopted.

The classification happens before drafting, before placement, and before any assumption that the discovery belongs in the Constitution.

The question is not:

"Does this feel important?"

The question is:

"Has this earned the Constitution?"

If the answer is uncertain, the discovery does not enter the Constitution.

It matures somewhere else.

## Required Gate

Every future discovery must answer these questions first.

### Question 1

Is this describing behavior or implementation?

If it describes implementation, it cannot enter the Constitution.

Implementation includes:

- Runtime mechanics.
- UI construction.
- Routing.
- Backend systems.
- Data structures.
- Framework choices.
- Technical ownership.
- Execution details.

Implementation can be documented, governed, and protected elsewhere.

It is not constitutional law.

### Question 2

Does this explain something broader than the feature that inspired it?

If no, do not place it in the Constitution.

A feature may reveal a principle, but the feature itself is not the principle.

The discovery must survive outside the original feature before it can be considered constitutional.

### Question 3

Has this discovery proven itself across multiple conversations?

Or is it still a fresh insight?

If it is still emerging, do not promote it yet.

Place it in review.

Fresh insight deserves protection from premature permanence.

## Discovery Classification

Every discovery must be placed into exactly one destination.

### 1. Constitution

Use the Constitution only for timeless principles that define how Meetro behaves.

Eligibility questions:

- Would this still matter fifty years from now?
- Would it remain true if today's technology disappeared?
- Does it define behavior rather than implementation?
- Does it protect purpose, character, stewardship, relationships, or truth?
- Has it survived repeated challenge?

If yes, it is eligible for constitutional review.

Eligibility is not adoption.

The Constitution should resist expansion.

### 2. Product Bible

Use the Product Bible for discoveries explaining how constitutional behavior becomes product behavior.

This includes:

- Interaction philosophy.
- Focus.
- Persistent context.
- Navigation.
- Workspace philosophy.
- Tool behavior.
- Product expression.
- Surface-level product judgment.

The Product Bible translates principles into product experience.

It does not make every product insight constitutional.

### 3. Steward's Journal

Use the Steward's Journal for historical discoveries and editorial memory.

This includes:

- Editorial lessons.
- Founder reflections.
- Moments that changed understanding.
- Writing evolution.
- Lessons learned during Constitution work.
- Context around why a decision mattered at the time.

The Steward's Journal preserves the path.

It is not permanent constitutional law.

### 4. Vault

Use the Vault for irreversible product truths and timeless observations discovered through experience.

Examples include:

- Law of Trust.
- Law of Interpretation.
- Law of Intent.
- Law of Scope.
- Future product laws.

The Vault preserves truths.

Not philosophy.

Vault entries may influence the Constitution, but they do not automatically become constitutional articles.

### 5. Architecture

Use Architecture for implementation-independent system organization.

This includes:

- Truth ownership.
- Projection.
- Identity.
- Lifecycle.
- Authority.
- System boundaries.
- Domain organization.

Architecture can be durable without being constitutional.

Architecture explains how truth is organized.

The Constitution explains what Meetro must remain.

### 6. Roadmap

Use the Roadmap for execution planning.

This includes:

- Execution order.
- Future implementation.
- Feature sequencing.
- Delivery planning.
- Release phases.
- Prioritization.

Roadmap discoveries are never constitutional.

The Roadmap decides when work happens.

The Constitution decides what must not be betrayed.

## Editorial Rule

Never say:

"This belongs in the Constitution."

Ask:

"Has it earned the Constitution?"

If uncertain, do not promote it.

Allow the discovery to mature.

The Constitution should resist expansion.

Not encourage it.

## Editorial Standard

The Constitution protects:

- Purpose.
- Character.
- Behavior.
- Stewardship.
- Relationships.
- Truth.

The Constitution does not protect:

- Features.
- Implementation.
- Architecture.
- Technology.
- Frameworks.
- Temporary discoveries.
- Execution plans.

Those belong elsewhere.

## Review State

When a discovery feels important but has not earned placement, mark it for review.

Review means:

- The discovery is preserved.
- The discovery is not promoted.
- The discovery is allowed to recur, mature, or fade.
- Future conversations may test it again.

Review is a disciplined state.

It prevents both loss and premature permanence.

## Final Editorial Principle

The Constitution is not a collection of discoveries.

It is the small number of discoveries that survived every challenge.

Future editions should remove more than they add.

The Constitution becomes stronger through discipline.

Not growth.

## Editorial Recommendation

The Constitution should become lighter with every edition.

Not heavier.
