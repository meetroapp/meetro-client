# Focus Workspace Foundation

People don't navigate their work.

They live inside it.

Meetro exists to preserve that continuity while quietly bringing the right tools to the right moment.

## 1. The Discovery

Phase 5 begins after Meetro discovered its next operating philosophy: continuity.

Earlier phases strengthened truth, identity, lifecycle, language, calmness, and stewardship. Those foundations made the next question visible: how should Meetro help people move through work without making the software feel like a place they have to constantly leave and re-enter?

The answer is continuity.

Meetro should not ask people to abandon the current work just to use another tool. It should preserve the relationship, the request, the job, and the current responsibility while changing only the focus required for the next useful action.

## 2. The Current Work Is The Anchor

The current work is the center of the experience.

The work remains.

Tools change.

Attention changes.

Context remains.

Current work may be a homeowner request, a professional job, an emergency, a scheduled visit, a proposal, an invoice, a completion review, or a historical record. The specific focus may change, but the person should always feel they are still inside the same work.

The work is not a page. It is the relationship and responsibility being carried forward.

## 3. The Law of Continuity

The current work is the anchor.

Context is never discarded.

Attention may change.

Tools may change.

The work remains.

Professionals should never leave their work to use the software.

The software should come to the work.

Continuity means that Meetro preserves the thread of meaning across tools. A conversation should not feel separate from a schedule. A proposal should not feel separate from the evaluation that created it. Completion should not feel separate from closure. History should not feel detached from the relationship it remembers.

## 4. The Law of Focus

Navigation provides orientation.

Focus provides progress.

Navigation answers: Where am I?

Focus answers: What deserves my attention right now?

Navigation remains useful, but it should not become the primary way people understand work. A person should not need to hunt through the product to decide the next responsible action. The current work should make the next focus visible.

## 5. Focus Workspace

Focus Workspace is the pattern that protects continuity.

It has two parts:

### Stable Context

Stable Context is the durable work anchor. It preserves the request, job, relationship, customer, business, lifecycle, and history that give the current moment meaning.

Stable Context answers:

- What work am I inside?
- Who is this with?
- What has already happened?
- What remains true while tools change?

### Dynamic Focus

Dynamic Focus is the part of the workspace that changes with the current responsibility.

Dynamic Focus may show conversation, schedule, evaluation, proposal, invoice, completion, closure, or history. It changes because attention changes, not because the user has left the work.

Dynamic Focus answers:

- What deserves attention now?
- What tool is needed now?
- What decision or action is next?

## 6. Portrait Philosophy

On iPhone portrait, do not force split view.

Small screens should preserve continuity through vertical rhythm, not through cramped side-by-side layouts.

Use:

- persistent Work Anchor
- changing Focus Area
- vertical continuity

The user should feel that the work remains present while the focus shifts below or within it. Portrait should feel calm, not compressed.

## 7. Landscape / Large Screen Philosophy

On landscape, iPad, Mac, and web, navigation may remain visible.

Focus may change beside it.

The philosophy remains the same.

Only presentation changes.

Large screens may support more simultaneous context, but they must not change ownership. The current work remains the anchor. Navigation may support orientation, and focus may appear beside stable context, but the underlying continuity model is unchanged.

## 8. Homeowner Application

The homeowner remains inside one request.

Proposal, schedule, conversation, arrival, completion, warranty, and history are chapters of the same work.

A homeowner should not feel like they are moving between disconnected tools. They should feel that their project is progressing and that Meetro is keeping the pieces together.

The homeowner experience should answer:

- What is happening with my request?
- What has the professional shared?
- What do I need to review, approve, confirm, or remember?

## 9. Professional Application

The professional remains inside one job.

Evaluation, proposal, invoice, completion, closure, and history are changing focus states, not disconnected destinations.

A professional should not feel like they are managing software modules. They should feel that Meetro is helping them keep the promise attached to the current job.

The professional experience should answer:

- What work am I responsible for?
- What needs my attention now?
- What tool should come to this job next?
- What must be preserved for the customer relationship?

## 10. Companion Relationship

The Companion protects awareness.

Focus Workspace protects context.

Together they protect continuity.

The Companion should notice what matters without owning the work. Focus Workspace should keep the person inside the work while the right tool appears at the right moment.

The Companion may observe, prepare, remind, and guide. It must not become the owner of workflow state, decisions, identity, or lifecycle truth.

## 11. Universal Lifecycle

The Universal Lifecycle becomes the timeline of the current work:

Relationship -> Communication -> Schedule -> Evaluation -> Proposal -> Approval -> Work -> Completion -> Closure -> History

This lifecycle is not a menu. It is the continuity thread that helps Meetro understand where attention belongs.

Each stage changes the focus. None of the stages discard context.

## 12. The Three Engines of Meetro

### Truth Engine

The Truth Engine protects what is real.

It owns:

- facts
- identity
- relationships
- history
- projections

The Truth Engine ensures that Meetro does not invent, duplicate, or fragment the work.

### Continuity Engine

The Continuity Engine protects where the person is.

It owns:

- context
- current work
- attention
- focus
- navigation

The Continuity Engine ensures that tools come to the work instead of forcing people to leave the work.

### Companion Engine

The Companion Engine protects awareness.

It owns:

- awareness
- preparation
- observation
- guidance
- never ownership
- never decisions

The Companion Engine helps the person see what matters while leaving control with the person and truth with the workflow.

## 13. What This Phase Is Not

This phase does not redesign:

- Business Profile
- Portfolio
- Work Center
- Messages
- Navigation

It defines the philosophy future implementation will follow.

Phase 5 is not a split-view implementation. It is not a new UI system. It is not a runtime behavior change. It is a constitutional architecture foundation for preserving continuity as Meetro grows.

## 14. Success Criteria

A professional should not ask:

Where do I go now?

They should feel:

I know what deserves my attention.

A homeowner should not ask:

Where is my quote?

They should feel:

I am still inside my project.

Success means people experience Meetro less as navigation and more as continuity.

## 15. Closing Principle

People don't navigate their work.

They live inside it.

Meetro exists to preserve that continuity while quietly bringing the right tools to the right moment.

The work remains.

The tools come to the work.
