# Spotlight Experience Standard

## Purpose

Spotlight is the emotional beginning of local discovery in Meetro.

Its responsibility is not to advertise services or rank businesses. Its responsibility is to help a homeowner imagine what meaningful local work can make possible.

Spotlight begins with story.

The professional follows the story.

The relationship begins from trust.

## Story First

Spotlight must answer one question before anything else:

What became possible because this work was completed?

The first impression should never be a category, a coupon, a service list, or a business card. The first impression should be a human outcome.

Examples:

- The kitchen where Sunday dinners began.
- The backyard where birthdays became traditions.
- The work that helped a family feel safe at home.
- The morning routine became peaceful again.

## Photography Rules

Spotlight photography should follow the Meetro Emotional Storytelling Standard.

Lead with:

- Families.
- Relationships.
- Celebration.
- Home life.
- Trust.
- Community.
- Life after the work.

Avoid leading with:

- Tools.
- Construction.
- Workers posing.
- Empty rooms.
- Technical completion.
- Before/after documentation without human meaning.

The completed work may support the story, but it should not become the story.

## Story And Photography Must Match

Every Spotlight must align:

Image

Headline

Caption

Professional

Action

All five pieces must tell the same story.

Examples:

- Kitchen dinner story uses kitchen, family, or shared meal photography.
- Pergola tradition story uses backyard, pergola, or family gathering photography.
- Leak repair peace-of-mind story uses plumbing, leak repair, or relief photography.
- Roof safety story uses roof, storm, or protected-home photography.
- Trust story uses homeowner/professional relationship photography.
- Community story uses neighbors, shared work, or community project photography.

If the image and headline contradict each other, Spotlight has failed even if each individual piece is beautiful.

## Visual Language

Spotlight cards should feel like a glimpse into a future memory.

Use:

- Large emotional imagery.
- Readable text overlays.
- Soft gradients for contrast.
- Calm spacing.
- Relationship-building calls to action.

Avoid:

- Directory grids.
- Advertising banners.
- Dense service cards.
- Coupon language.
- Ranking language.
- Social feed behavior.

## Relationship-First Discovery

Business identity belongs after the story.

Use language like:

"Meet the professional who helped make it possible."

The action should encourage trust and relationship, not browsing a listing.

Preferred actions:

- Meet the Professional.
- View Their Story.
- Explore Their Work.

Avoid:

- Review Profile.
- View Listing.
- Book Now.
- Shop Services.

## Community Inspiration

Spotlight should help homeowners feel that their community contains people who can help make home safer, calmer, and more connected.

It is not a marketplace feed.

It is not a directory.

It is not paid placement.

It is a story-led introduction to meaningful local work.

## Connection to Meetro Moments

Spotlight is the beginning of the emotional arc that Meetro Moments preserves.

Spotlight shows what could become possible.

Meetro Moments preserves what actually happened.

Spotlight should also lead naturally toward Communication, Work, and Meetro Moments:

- Communication begins when the homeowner wants to meet the professional.
- Work begins when trust becomes a request.
- Meetro Moments preserve what the completed work made possible.

Both must protect the same principle:

Work creates history.

History strengthens relationships.

## Prohibited Patterns

Do not turn Spotlight into:

- A service catalog.
- An ad placement.
- A coupon carousel.
- A ranking board.
- A random business feed.
- A social post stream.
- A technical portfolio dump.

If Spotlight begins to feel like software discovery instead of human possibility, it has drifted.

## Success Standard

Spotlight succeeds when a homeowner pauses because they can imagine their own life becoming better.

The user should feel:

"That could become my story."

Then, only after that feeling, Meetro introduces the professional who helped make it possible.
